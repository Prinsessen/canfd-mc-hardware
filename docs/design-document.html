CANFD-MC Adapter

A minimal board for the Indian Springfield bus: ESP32-S3-WROOM-1U + MCP2518FD, soldered wires, no USB connector, no isolation, no termination. The firmware from Prinsessen/indian-thunderstroke-canbus (environment sniffer-t2can) runs unchanged.

Pin numbers were looked up in LilyGO's schematic T-2Can-Fd_V1.0.pdf, Espressif's WROOM-1 datasheet and TI SNVSAU4D — not from memory. The buck values in §4.5 were calculated from the datasheet equations and confirmed in WEBENCH.

1Block diagram

One ground throughout. Supply and bus come from the same service connector on the bike, so LilyGO's isolated TD501MCANFD module (and its internal DC/DC, which was three quarters of the 17 mA sleep current) is gone. The only parts that are genuinely redrawn compared with LilyGO are the power supply and the bus front end; the MCP2518FD section is a 1:1 copy.

2Module choices

ESP32-S3-WROOM-1U-N16R8 — the same module as the T-2CANFD, so platformio.ini (esp32-s3-devkitc-1, 16 MB flash / 8 MB OPI PSRAM) and the partition table are untouched. The module has a U.FL connector instead of a PCB antenna, which is the right choice on a motorcycle anyway.

Temperature: the N16R8 is only rated −40…+65 °C ambient

The 8 MB octal PSRAM is the limit. Under a seat in the sun, 65 °C is not much margin. Review finding (2026-09-15): the firmware is built for the PlatformIO board esp32-s3-devkitc-1, which is the "N8, no PSRAM" definition — the PSRAM is never initialised and only 8 MB of flash is used. So the +85 °C variants N8 or N16R2 need no firmware change. Rev 1.0 was built with the N16R8 before this was noticed; keep those boards out of the hottest spots. Rev 1.1: order an 85 °C module. Same footprint.

MCP2518FD in SOIC-14, 3.3 V, 40 MHz crystal — exactly as LilyGO. The firmware runs the controller in Normal20B (classic CAN + 29-bit IDs) or ListenOnly; CAN FD is not used, but the chip is the one the code and ACAN2517FD know.

TCAN332G — 3.3 V transceiver, SOIC-8, −40…+125 °C, no extra pins to wire. Listen-only is handled in the MCP2518FD, so the transceiver's silent pin (TCAN337G) is unnecessary. Drop-in alternatives with the same base pinout: TCAN337G, SN65HVD230.

LM5164 — 100 V, 1 A synchronous buck with ~10 µA quiescent current. Survives load dump with the TVS as the only protection, and the quiescent current matters when the board hangs permanently on the battery.

4.3Oscillator: 40 MHz crystal — settled by your own firmware

  • 40 MHz, not 20. can_hal_mcp.cpp sets MCP_OSC_HZ 40000000UL and ACAN2517FDSettings::OSC_40MHz. ACAN2517FD derives its bit timing from that frequency; had the crystal been 20 MHz, 250 kbit/s would have been configured as 125, and you would read zero frames. You read frames (live J1939 from the Zealand ride, version 2026.09.14-x). Running firmware beats vendor examples.
  • LilyGO's own can example is a trap. It calls begin() without a clock argument, and Longan_CANFD defaults to 20 MHz — so the example cannot work on their own board. LilyGO's wiki itself notes that it "requires CAN FD adaptation".
  • It is a crystal, not an oscillator module. LilyGO's schematic shows X1 "40MHZ ±10ppm" on OSC1/OSC2 with C16/C17 = 12 pF; the MCP2518FD's internal Pierce oscillator drives it. Cheaper and more vibration-tolerant than an active module. LilyGO's 2 × 12 pF suit a crystal with CL ≈ 9–10 pF; the crystal JLCPCB stocks (X322540MPB4SI) has CL = 15 pF, so here C10/C11 = 22 pF: C = 2·(CL − Cstray) = 2·(15 − 3).

Small correction for your repo

The comment in can_hal_mcp.cpp line 17 says "Longan_CANFD + ACAN2517FD both assume 40 MHz here". That is only true of ACAN2517FD — Longan defaults to 20. The conclusion is right; the premise can send the next reader the wrong way.

4.4Termination: DO NOT POPULATE

The bike's bus already has its 2 × 120 Ω, and the board sits on a stub. A third termination would pull the bus down to 40 Ω and cause reflections. RT1/RT2/CT1 stay as empty footprints marked BENCH ONLY on the silkscreen — they exist only for the day the board sits on the bench against a USB-CAN adapter without a real bus, when both ends need terminating.

What you actually need to watch is the stub length. At 250 kbit/s you have about a metre from the tap point to the board before reflections start to cost. Keep it short, and keep the twist of the CAN pair right up to P3/P4.

4.5LM5164 — calculated values CONFIRMED IN WEBENCH

Calculated from TI SNVSAU4D (rev. Feb 2026), section 7.2.2 and table 6-1. Assumptions: VOUT = 3.3 V, VIN nominal 13.8 V, VIN 6–40 V, IOUT dimensioned for 1 A (actual < 0.5 A — WiFi TX bursts dominate).

Switching frequency
300 kHz → RRON = 27.4 kΩ
RRON [kΩ] = VOUT · 2500 / FSW [kHz] = 3.3·2500/300 = 27.5 (eq. 15). The 50 ns minimum on-time gives VIN,max = 3.3/(50 ns·300 kHz) = 220 V — no constraint.
Inductor
22 µH
L = VOUT/(FSW·ΔIL)·(1−VOUT/VIN) with ΔIL = 0.4 A at 13.8 V → 20.9 µH (eq. 20). At 40 V: ΔIL = 0.46 A. Ipeak ≈ 1.2 A at 1 A load → Isat ≥ 1.5 A.
Output capacitor
2 × 22 µF
COUT ≥ ΔIL/(8·FSW·ΔVOUT) = 0.4/(8·300k·16.5 mV) ≈ 10 µF (eq. 21). Doubled for DC-bias derating.
Feedback divider
RFB1 100 kΩ · RFB2 57.6 kΩ
RFB2 = 1.2 V/(VOUT−1.2 V)·RFB1 = 57.1 kΩ (eq. 10). E96: 57.6 kΩ → VOUT = 1.2·(1+100/57.6) = 3.283 V.
Ripple injection, type 3
RA 150 kΩ · CA 2.2 nF · CB 270 pF
CA ≥ 10/(FSW·RFB1∥RFB2) = 0.91 nF (eq. 24). RA·CA ≤ tON·(VIN−VOUT)/20 mV; tON = 797 ns at 13.8 V → gives 25 mV at 13.8 V, 15 mV at 6 V (min. 12 mV ✓), 31 mV at 40 V. CB ≥ 75 µs/(3·RFB1) = 250 pF (eq. 26), C0G.
UVLO
RUV1 365 kΩ · RUV2 100 kΩ
VIN,on = 1.5·(1+RUV1/RUV2) = 6.98 V; VIN,off = 1.4·(1+…) = 6.51 V (eq. 13/14). Below 6 V the LM5164 is out of spec anyway. During a cranking dip the board restarts — the wake path is already robust to that.
Input
2 × 4.7 µF 100 V + CBST 2.2 nF
Datasheet: ≥ 2.2 µF HF ceramic, voltage rating ≥ 2 × VIN,max. The battery leads are > 5 cm, so D2 + ceramics sit right at U3.
Sleep current, expected
≈ 7–9 mA from 12 V
MCP2518FD in Normal (~10–20 mA) + TCAN332G recessive (~6 mA) at 3.3 V, LM5164 ~10 µA. Through the buck ≈ half of the 17 mA measured on the LilyGO — the isolated DC/DC is gone. Measure it.

WEBENCH run 2026-09-15 (VIN 6–40 V, VOUT 3.3 V, IOUT 1 A, 60 °C): TI's tool picks RON 27.4 kΩ, L 22 µH and CBST 2.2 nF — identical to the above. Feedback 178k/101k (same ratio as 100k/57.6k), Cout 3 × 10 µF, ripple network 205k/1.2 nF/100 pF — equivalent to ours, which injects slightly less ripple and still ≥ 12 mV at 6 V. Efficiency 78 % at 1 A.

Type 3 was chosen because the output is pure ceramic with no ESR — types 1/2 would need a series resistor and more ripple on 3V3.

5.2Placement (as built)

  1. U1 at the left end, U.FL connector facing out over the board edge. The WROOM-1U has no PCB antenna, so there is no keep-out beyond the module's own edge — but the buck and its inductor are kept away from it.
  2. U3 + L2 + C1/C2 + C3/C4 at the right end. The hot loop (C1 → U3 VIN → SW → L2 → C4 → GND) is the smallest area on the board. SW node: only on L1, short and wide.
  3. U2 and X1 top centre. Crystal within 5 mm of OSC1/OSC2, C10/C11 between crystal and U2, GND return straight to pin 7.
  4. U4 → L1 → D3 → pads in a straight line down the middle. D3 on the bus side of L1, closest to the pads.
  5. SPI on the back, ≤ 25 mm, over unbroken GND.

5.3The seven rules

  1. CANH/CANL routed as a pair, 0.3 mm trace, matched length, no vias between U4 and the pads.
  2. Decoupling capacitors within 2 mm of the pin they decouple; 100 nF closest.
  3. U3's EP and U1's EP: via arrays into L2.
  4. FB node (R2/R3/C7) short and away from SW and L2. The RA/CA branch may run along SW — it is meant to see SW.
  5. VBAT path from P1 through F1 → D1 → D2/C1 → U3: 0.8 mm wide on top, 0.4 mm on the back run. 3V3 plane on L3.
  6. Signal name at every solder pad on the silkscreen. CANH/CANL can be swapped with solder pads — they could not with a keyed connector.
  7. No component closer than 1.5 mm to the board edge (coating and edge milling).

6Mechanical and environment

  • All SMD, no pin headers, no electrolytics, no screw terminals.
  • −40/+105 °C parts; AEC-Q200 passives where they cost nothing extra. The exception is U1 (§2).
  • Conformal coating after the first flash and test — or soft silicone potting. Not epoxy: Murata's spec for L1 (§13.4) warns that cure stress from resin can change the inductance or break the wire; the same caution applies to X1. Keep the U.FL connector and the pads free during testing.
  • No mounting holes: the board is coated or potted, and fixed with foam tape and a cable tie, or potted in a small box.
  • Antenna: a short stub antenna on a U.FL pigtail, placed clear of metal and carbon fibre. A PCB antenna under a seat behind steel does not work.

6.0Soldered wires — pads, no connectors

The service connector provides CAN H, CAN L, GND and permanent 12 V. Because the board is not isolated there is one ground: LilyGO's five wires (12 V+, 12 V−, D GND, CAN H, CAN L) become four — VBAT, GND, CANH, CANL — along the bottom edge.

Strain-relief holes — the difference between lasting a season and lasting years

A solder joint on a vibrating vehicle breaks at the root of the wire, not in the solder. The fix costs nothing: an extra Ø1.2 mm unplated hole 3 mm from each pad. The wire comes in from the edge, up through the hole, and down into the pad, so the board takes the strain instead of the joint. Add a drop of glue or resin over each pad after soldering.

The CAN shield is not bonded on the board — it is already grounded at the ECM (single point). So there is no S GND pad.

7.1Notes for rev 1.1

From the review of rev 1.0. None of these blocks bring-up of rev 1.0.

  • Module: N8 or N16R2 (85 °C) instead of N16R8. No firmware change.
  • Leftover ACC_SENSE label on IO1 — removed in the repository after the order; the fabricated rev 1.0 has an unconnected pad there, which is harmless.
  • BOM text for C3/C4/C12/C13 said 10 V; the ordered part (C45783) is 25 V. Text corrected.
  • Cranking dip: UVLO releases at 6.5 V; a cold crank can dip below that and reboot the board (the wake path tolerates it). Measure the dip during cranking before rev 1.1 is frozen. If it matters, the fix is a regulator with a lower minimum input, not a bigger capacitor.
  • One-ground rule: flashing over the USB pads must happen with the board off the bike, or from a laptop on battery — a mains-powered PC's USB ground to the bike's ground is the one case the dropped isolation would have covered.

Candidate board changes for rev 1.1 — none justify a new order on their own; take them along when the bench gives a reason:

  • Test pads for +3V3 and VIN_PROT on the back, next to the flash pads — measuring on 0603 capacitor pads is a poor way to debug on a bike.
  • RT1/RT2 as 1206 with more room around them: they are fitted and removed by hand for bench testing.
  • An unpopulated 1.27 mm header footprint (or wider pads) in place of the pogo pads, so a header can be press-fitted for the first flash without a fixture.
  • If the cranking measurement shows reboots: a regulator with a lower minimum input (LM5163 / LM61460 class) or a lower UVLO — a schematic change, so wait for the measurement.
